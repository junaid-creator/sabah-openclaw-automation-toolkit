# Same-job generated-poster revision and failed-review audit

Use when a generated reference-workflow poster failed image accuracy or text-fit QA, a technical render retry invalidated its hash-bound review, or an existing failed semantic review needs an evidence audit, while its job/request and scene checkpoint already exist. This is neither a new campaign nor a supplied-photo resize. A correction request is not approval to publish; the original creation/publish-intent message does not approve changed media. For owner-supplied media use [supplied-image intake](../supplied-image-intake/SKILL.md), and for any approval receipt or platform attempt use [publication recovery](../multi-platform-publication-recovery/SKILL.md).

1. Reconcile the existing request ID, campaign, selected template, captions, target platforms, scene checkpoint and latest hash-bound semantic/quality review. Inspect actual scene JPEGs and rendered poster, not just objective metrics: a mixer can pass decode/layout checks while depicting a different mechanism or model across panels. Check the queue is an unapproved MANUAL draft and inspect approval, schedule, operation and post ledgers before changing any media. Before starting a controller revision, check per-request lock contention and active controller/scene-generator processes with their job/version/log: another session may already be revising the same job, even when the draft still points to the older preview. If work is active, coordinate ownership and await its saved checkpoint/output; do not queue a competing revision or alter shared renderer files beneath it. A READY scene alone is not permission to replace it. If a generation is RUNNING/UNCERTAIN, reconcile that provider task and saved output first; never blindly submit another. Before rendering a corrected version or requesting more media, inspect the installed reviewer’s poster-path selection, attempt/receipt lock key, quality gate and publisher selection: a renderer that saves a versioned JPEG does not establish that the reviewer can independently admit it under the same job. If the installed reviewer fixes the unversioned path and refuses any prior attempt for the job, preserve the failed attempt and stop at isolated software/render QA unless an authorized, tested version-aware admission route exists; do not clear the lock, reuse the old review or invent a new job to obtain another inference.
2. Freeze the old checkpoint, failed review, poster, atlas and selected scene files as a scoped revision archive. Preserve immutable provider sources, request/job ID, explicit template, owner copy, captions, targets and publication history. Distinguish a text-fit failure from an image failure: if finalization stopped before upload/review, shorten only the overflowing campaign copy within the existing job (never silently alter owner-supplied exact copy or shared template geometry), retain the READY scene checkpoint, and render to a new versioned poster path. The campaign-content hash change invalidates any old semantic review; inspect the final version, but bind a fresh review to its actual campaign, scene and poster hashes only through the supported, independently verified version-aware admission route from step 1. Otherwise keep the candidate isolated and report the admission blocker. Do not regenerate images for copy-only correction. For an image failure, compare the original scene → assembled atlas → final decoded JPEG before choosing the repair layer, even when the independent reviewer returned PASS. A complete source can lose its handle/base during atlas assembly: `ImageOps.fit` crops to the target aspect ratio, and a downstream `object-fit:contain` cannot recover those missing pixels. If this is the evidenced cause, preserve the READY scenes and fixed template; test a contain/pad assembly for the affected region in a new versioned atlas/poster rather than regenerating photography or changing CSS. Inspect the final mask treatment too: preserving atlas bounds does not prove the exported subject is complete. If assembly is intact and the template itself clips or masks required content, correct only the authorized geometry/crop in isolation. Preserve the original review, record any visual disagreement separately, and require fresh exact-artifact review for the materially changed output; never rewrite an old PASS or FAIL. A successful isolated correction does not establish that the installed assembler uses it. If a scene itself is wrong, correct only the rejected scene indices using the frozen campaign brief and a concrete visual-continuity instruction; a generated scene does not independently prove product ratings. Bound correction attempts to the authorized limit (the verified reference-workflow path used at most two *additional* generations per scene); exhausting that limit holds QA rather than accepting a bad image. A reviewed local replacement must have its source hash and decoded image recorded, and should not overwrite the archived predecessor. Reject stale review/checkpoint hashes or a second correction command that would replay the same rejection.
3. Render the same template without replacing a reviewed poster at its existing pathname. For an unchanged technical retry, reuse the exact output only when the renderer’s binding of source atlas, campaign copy, style/code, CSS, branding and fonts still matches and the recorded output SHA256 equals the file bytes; a cached flag alone is insufficient. If an input or output hash differs, stop the retry from overwriting that artifact and use a new versioned pathname under the same job for an authorized correction. Preserve the prior JPEG, render QA and semantic review. Run objective checks on the actual selected version: decoded dimensions, copy and supplied specifications, layout, logo, QR destination and footer. Inspect that final poster **and all scenes** for product identity, mechanism, visual continuity, invented labels and unrelated equipment. Before invoking the independent reviewer, compare its expected-copy list with the actual visible final poster: renderer QA may collect `.campaign-copy` text from elements hidden by a template-specific `display:none`. Filter the review evidence to visible rendered text (check computed display/visibility and visible bounds), while keeping the objective renderer QA and original campaign copy intact. An expectation from hidden DOM text can produce a genuine FAIL despite visually sound output. Preserve its attempt and verdict; do not infer a PASS from otherwise positive checkboxes. Before an authorized audit of that failure, compare the original reviewed JPEG and scene/campaign hashes with the saved binding, model answer and actual submission provenance. A pre-inference binding alone is not proof of submitted bytes: inspect the original inference envelope or attestation and whether post-inference hash verification ran even on FAIL. If the required provenance is missing, do not create a reconciliation PASS or repeat inference on identical bytes; keep the ordinary review lock and report the exact gap. Correct the visible-text evidence contract, then admit only an independently reviewed exact artifact under the applicable authorization (or a separately supported, fully evidenced audited reconciliation), without rewriting the original FAIL. For a genuinely new review, save its semantic receipt bound to its campaign/scene/poster hashes; for an authorized reconciliation, retain the original FAIL and bind a separate audit record to the original exact hashes. Never edit the old rejection into a pass. For a versioned poster, compare the selected pointer, local bytes, public upload, Sheet platform assets and delivered review hash. Then run the **installed publisher’s quality gate**, not only the draft controller’s version-specific QA: a publisher that defaults to the unversioned first poster can report `EXACT_CONTENT_QA_REQUIRED` even while the current version passes. If that mismatch is proven, correct the gate to resolve the immutable selected poster within the job directory, verify its recorded SHA256 before `require_pass(job, selected_path)`, and retain legacy fallback only when no selected version exists. Re-run the production gate against the exact approved hash before any submit; do not rewrite the review or suppress QA to pass. A technical rerun after SEMANTIC_REVIEW_REQUIRED_OR_STALE must reuse the same job/request without bypassing the hash-bound gate or generating a duplicate job.
4. Resume the canonical draft controller under its existing request ID only after quality passes. Verify the *existing* Sheets job/content row and every platform asset URL, caption, target and status by readback; the job must remain DRAFT / MANUAL / WAITING_APPROVAL. A public preview URL is not a platform post URL. If a platform needs a proportional derivative, preserve the original, validate decoded dimensions and public bytes against that platform's installed validator, and bind the platform-specific URL/hash to the exact review. Do not equate source-byte identity with recipient-byte proof or silently change media after review.
5. For each requested owner channel, follow [automation adapter verification](../automation-adapter-verification/SKILL.md): claim the new exact-content binding, invoke the messaging tool once, persist the raw structured receipt, and validate recipient/account/message ID. Keep prior review and independent channel receipts; never resend CLAIMED/UNKNOWN or treat pending_tool_delivery as success. A corrected poster should continue to review through this handoff rather than stop at local QA. One later authenticated approval of the frozen final content covers its frozen targets; verify the durable approval receipt before invoking the existing immediate or scheduled publisher. A scheduled request also needs its recorded due-time authorization. No approval receipt means stop at review, even if the original request said “publish now.”
6. Test with temporary state and fake provider/Sheets/platform clients: stale rejection, READY/RUNNING/UNCERTAIN checkpoint, bounded attempts, identical job ID, archived predecessor, changed content invalidating old review, one receipt per channel, approval gating, scheduled due registration, partial platform success and duplicate-safe recovery. Run installed quality, schedule, approval and publisher regressions separately. Label local simulations as such; verify actual Gateway/plugin generation and worker timers independently. An enabled plugin or passed unit test is not proof that changed inbound routing loaded or that a live owner approval was exercised.

Completion: for a review audit blocked on provenance, preserve the original FAIL and report the exact missing evidence without admitting the poster. For a completed revision, require the same job and saved history, current hash-bound QA PASS, final media and platform-dimension evidence, Sheets draft readback, genuine preview receipts, and either a durable exact-content approval with per-platform public-URL verification or the precise missing owner-approval gate. Never claim live publishing from a successful draft or test suite.
